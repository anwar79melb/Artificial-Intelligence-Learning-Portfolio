# Tutorial 11: Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)

## Overview

In the previous tutorial, we explored Autoencoders and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), which learn latent representations of data and can generate new samples from a learned latent space.

This tutorial introduces **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)**, one of the most influential breakthroughs in generative artificial intelligence. GANs consist of two neural networks competing against each other in a game-like process:

- Generator (G)
- Discriminator (D)

The Generator attempts to create realistic data samples, while the Discriminator attempts to distinguish real samples from generated ones. Through this competition, both networks improve until the Generator can produce highly realistic outputs. 【1-7eca8d】

GANs have enabled major advances in:

- Face generation
- Image synthesis
- Deepfakes
- Image enhancement
- Style transfer
- Data augmentation
- Creative AI systems

# 1. What are Generative Adversarial Networks?

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are a class of generative models introduced to create realistic synthetic data. 【1-7eca8d】

Unlike traditional neural networks that make predictions:

```text
Input
   ↓
Prediction
```

GANs learn:

```text
Input Noise
      ↓
Generate New Data
```

that resembles the training dataset. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 2. The Core Idea Behind GANs

GANs consist of two competing neural networks:

```text
Generator (G)

Discriminator (D)
```

These models are trained simultaneously. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Generator

The Generator:

```text
Noise Vector
      ↓
Synthetic Sample
```

creates fake data that attempts to resemble real data. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Discriminator

The Discriminator:

```text
Input Sample
      ↓
Real or Fake?
```

acts as a classifier.

Its job is to determine whether an input comes from:

```text
Real Dataset

or

Generator
```【1-7eca8d】

---

# 3. Adversarial Learning

GAN training can be viewed as a game.

---

## Generator Goal

The Generator wants to:

```text
Fool the Discriminator
```

by producing realistic fake samples. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Discriminator Goal

The Discriminator wants to:

```text
Detect Fake Data
```

correctly. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Competition

```text
Generator
      ↓
Creates Fake Samples
      ↓
Discriminator
      ↓
Identifies Real/Fake
```

Both models improve through competition.

---

# 4. GAN Architecture

The overall architecture is:

```text
Random Noise (z)
          ↓
      Generator
          ↓
     Fake Sample
          ↓
     Discriminator
          ↓
   Real or Fake?
```

Real samples from the dataset are also fed to the Discriminator during training. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 5. Random Noise Input

The Generator does not receive images.

Instead, it receives:

```text
Random Noise Vector
```

commonly sampled from:

```math
z \sim N(0,1)
```

This noise serves as the starting point for generation. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 6. Training Objective

The overall GAN objective is:

```text
Generator:
Create realistic fakes

Discriminator:
Detect fakes
```

The ideal outcome occurs when:

```text
Generated Distribution
=
Real Data Distribution
```

and the Discriminator can no longer reliably distinguish between them. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 7. GAN Loss Functions

GANs use adversarial loss functions.

The Generator and Discriminator have opposing objectives. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 8. Discriminator Loss

The Discriminator attempts to:

### Classify Real Samples as Real

and

### Classify Fake Samples as Fake

The objective is to maximize its classification performance. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Desired Behaviour

```text
Real Image
      ↓
Output = 1

Fake Image
      ↓
Output = 0
```

---

# 9. Generator Loss

The Generator attempts to:

```text
Produce Fake Images
```

that the Discriminator classifies as real. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Desired Behaviour

```text
Fake Image
      ↓
Discriminator says REAL
```

This forces the Generator to produce increasingly realistic outputs.

---

# 10. GAN Training Process

Training alternates between both networks.

---

## Step 1

Sample random noise:

```text
z
```

---

## Step 2

Generate fake images.

```text
G(z)
```

---

## Step 3

Train the Discriminator using:

- Real images
- Generated images

---

## Step 4

Update the Discriminator.

---

## Step 5

Train the Generator.

---

## Step 6

Repeat.

This process continues for many epochs. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 11. Generating New Data

Once training is complete:

```text
Noise Vector
      ↓
Generator
      ↓
New Synthetic Sample
```

The Discriminator is no longer required.

Only the Generator is used. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 12. GANs as Distribution Transformers

GANs learn a transformation from:

```text
Simple Distribution
```

to:

```text
Complex Data Distribution
```

For example:

```text
Gaussian Noise
      ↓
Face Images
```

or:

```text
Gaussian Noise
      ↓
Handwritten Digits
```【1-7eca8d】

# 13. Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGANs)

Basic GANs may struggle with large images.

To improve performance, researchers developed:

```text
Deep Convolutional GANs
(DCGANs)
```

which use CNNs inside both the Generator and Discriminator. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 14. DCGAN Architecture Principles

Researchers proposed several guidelines.

---

## Replace Pooling Layers

Use:

```text
Strided Convolutions
```

in the Discriminator. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Generator Upsampling

Use:

```text
Transposed Convolutions
```

to increase image resolution. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Batch Normalization

Apply Batch Normalization to improve:

- Stability
- Convergence
- Performance【1-7eca8d】

---

## Remove Fully Connected Layers

Deep convolutional structures generally perform better. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Activation Functions

### Generator

Use:

```text
ReLU
```

except for the output layer.

---

### Output Layer

Use:

```text
Tanh
```

---

### Discriminator

Use:

```text
Leaky ReLU
```

throughout the network. 【1-7eca8d】

---

# 15. Building a DCGAN

A typical DCGAN contains:

---

## Generator

```text
Noise Vector
      ↓
Dense Layer
      ↓
Transposed Conv Layers
      ↓
Generated Image
```

---

## Discriminator

```text
Input Image
      ↓
Conv Layers
      ↓
Binary Classification
```

GAN training may require many hours or days.

TensorFlow allows:

```text

# 19. Challenges in GAN Training

GANs are powerful but difficult to train.

Common issues:

### Mode Collapse

Generator produces limited diversity.

---

### Training Instability

Generator and Discriminator may not learn at the same pace.

---

### Convergence Problems

Finding equilibrium can be difficult.

# 20. Progressive Growing GANs

In 2018, researchers proposed:

```text
Progressive Growing of GANs
```【1-7eca8d】

---

## Key Idea

Start training with:

```text
Small Images
```

and gradually increase image resolution during training. 【1-7eca8d】

---

## Benefits

✅ Faster training

✅ More stable learning

✅ High-resolution image generation

✅ Improved visual quality

---

# 21. Progressive GAN Workflow

```text
Low Resolution Images
          ↓
Train GAN
          ↓
Add New Layers
          ↓
Increase Resolution
          ↓
Continue Training
```

Previously learned parameters are retained. 【1-7eca8d】
